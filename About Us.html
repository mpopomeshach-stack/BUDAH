Our Heritage Sites

Discover the caves, people and history that make Lesotho's heritage remarkable.

Liphofung Cave

Liphofung, meaning “place of the eland”, is a cultural and environmental heritage site in the Botha-Bothe district of Lesotho. Nestled beneath a massive sandstone overhang, the site holds centuries of history.

The cave once sheltered San hunter-gatherers, who left behind rock paintings. Later, it became a resting place for King Moshoeshoe I and his people.

Our Mission & Community

Today, Liphofung helps preserve Lesotho's history, promote cultural tourism and support the local community through guided tours, traditional craft displays and educational exhibits.

Masitise Cave

Masitise Cave House Museum, in Quthing, is a historical site built directly beneath a massive rock shelter. Originally used by San people, it became a permanent home in 1866–1867 when Swiss Protestant missionary Reverend David-Frédéric Ellenberger built brick walls beneath the overhang after being displaced during the Seqiti War.

Today the residence operates as a cultural museum showcasing nineteenth-century missionary life, Basotho history, fading San rock art and impressions of ancient dinosaur footprints.

Our Mission & Community

The museum preserves nineteenth-century history, protects prehistoric rock art and dinosaur footprints, and promotes cultural tourism in Quthing.

Kome Cave

Kome's cave dwellings are an important part of Lesotho's cultural heritage.

Our Mission

The dwellings originally served as a hideout from adversaries during a period of drought in the late eighteenth century.
